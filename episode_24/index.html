Air gives up at about 3,000 volts for every millimeter. Guess how far away the spark will jump, then reach.
Game 1 is the charge pump from the video: every step on the carpet rubs a few hundred volts onto you; summer air carries most of it away between steps, a heated winter room (around 20% humidity) keeps nearly all of it, and you feel a static spark from about 3,000 volts up. Game 2: air breaks down at roughly 3,000 volts per millimeter, so a 6,000-volt finger sparks across about 2 mm before it touches. Numbers are illustrative and rounded; the spark is tiny in energy, which is why it stings and does nothing else.
