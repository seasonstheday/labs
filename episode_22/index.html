Game 1 is the sonar plate from the video: sound moves about 1,125 feet a second, so an echo from a moth 3 feet away is back in about 5.3 thousandths of a second; distance = speed of sound × echo time ÷ 2. The game slows the sound down 190 times so you can watch it. Real bats click about 20 times a second while searching and more than 150 times a second as they close in. Game 2 is the night-shift plate: day-hunting hawks stop around sunset, insects in the air peak about an hour after it, and the lines cross at about 6:20 pm on a late-October evening. Scientists think that trade-off is why bats leave when they do; the curves here are illustrative, not measured. A bat on the ground in the daytime may be sick: never touch it, tell a grown-up.
