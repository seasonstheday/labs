# Myth fact-check, build 50

October 2026. Every myth's card was checked, the shaky ones against sources. 43 of the original 66 were fine as written. 23 were reworded; none were wrong enough to remove. Then 78 new myths were added, for 144 in all.

## The 23 corrections

| Myth | What changed |
|---|---|
| `bull` | Bulls can see colour, just not red against green. "Can't see red" overstated it. |
| `sugar` | The "birthday parties" line read as a finding. Studies point at excitement and expectations, so it is softened. |
| `bat` | Not every bat echolocates. Many fruit bats rely on their eyes. |
| `tongue` | Named the actual source: a 1901 German paper that was misread, not a mistranslated book. |
| `knuckle` | The bubble forms when the joint pops; it is not a bubble sitting there. The doctor found no arthritis in either hand. |
| `camel` | Camels don't keep water "in their blood". They conserve it through the whole body. |
| `penny` | Sources disagree on the speed (from about 40 to over 60 mph), so the number is gone. Everyone agrees it can't badly hurt you. |
| `words` | "Ten words a day" was too precise. Estimates are a few thousand words a year. |
| `spiders` | "Not one recorded case" can't be proved. Now "no evidence". |
| `bolt` | The Empire State Building is hit about 20 to 25 times a year, so "over 20". |
| `frogdrink` | "Don't drink at all" was too strong. They don't drink with their mouths. |
| `dragonfly` | "Hundreds a day" is the top of the estimates. Now "lots". |
| `napoleon` | About 1.69 m, not 1.68. |
| `shark` | Some sharks (great whites, makos) really do need to keep swimming. The myth now says "every shark" and the fact says some do. |
| `pyramid` | Tidied: the workers' tombs are near the pyramids, not "beside the king's". |
| `polarbear` | It is pregnant females that den, not "mothers with new cubs". |
| `lemming` | The film crew herded the lemmings; "pushed" was a guess. |
| `northpole` | The ice is about 2.7 km thick, so "nearly 3 km" rather than "two miles". |
| `penguin` | Galápagos penguins live on the equator, and a few just north of it. |
| `monkey` | It was one zoo (Paignton, England), not "zoos". |
| `sloth` | The "three times faster swimming" claim has no solid source, so it's dropped. |
| `shooting` | Most shooting stars come from grains the size of sand, smaller than a pea. |
| `moon` | Moon rock resembles Earth rocks in general, not specifically the crust. |

## The 78 new myths

Each one lists the source Dad names in the Diary. They are added at the end of each chapter's list, so the first stages of every chapter play as before. Each stage now brings in the next slice of the chapter's myths (the newest eight plus two from the chapter before), so every myth appears before the boss.

### The Backyard (+13)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🪱 | Cut an earthworm in half and you get two worms. | You get one hurt worm. Sometimes the head end grows a new tail, but the tail end can't grow a new head. | Natural History Museum |
| 🕸️ | Daddy longlegs are the most venomous spiders in the world. | Most daddy longlegs aren't spiders at all, and they have no venom. The spiders that share the name have venom far too weak to hurt you. | University of California, Riverside |
| 🐝 | Science says bumblebees shouldn't be able to fly. | That idea came from a quick sum that treated bee wings like stiff aeroplane wings. Real bee wings flap and twist, stirring up tiny whirlwinds that lift them. | Scientific American |
| 🌼 | Every bee dies after it stings. | Only honeybee workers lose their stinger, because it has hooks that catch in skin. Bumblebees and most other bees can sting again. | Natural History Museum |
| 🐣 | A mother bird abandons a chick that a person has touched. | Most birds have a poor sense of smell and won't notice. Still, the kindest thing is to leave chicks where they are. | Audubon |
| 🐕 | One dog year is the same as seven human years. | Dogs grow up fast: a one-year-old dog is more like a teenager. After that they age more slowly, and big dogs age faster than small ones. | American Veterinary Medical Association |
| 🐶 | Dogs only see in black and white. | Dogs see blues and yellows. Reds and greens look alike to them, a bit like they do for some colour-blind people. | American Kennel Club |
| 🌻 | Sunflowers turn to follow the Sun all their lives. | Only young sunflowers follow the Sun. Grown-up flowers stop and face east, where the morning sun warms them and brings five times more bees. | UC Berkeley |
| 🐞 | A ladybird's spots tell you how old it is. | The spots show what kind of ladybird it is. A ladybird keeps the same spots its whole life. | Natural History Museum |
| 🪲 | Earwigs crawl into people's ears. | Earwigs hide under bark and stones and eat plants and tiny bugs. They have no interest in your ears. | Royal Horticultural Society |
| 🍭 | Swallowed gum stays in your tummy for seven years. | Your body can't break gum down, but it moves through and comes out in a few days. Still, gum is for chewing! | Mayo Clinic |
| 🍉 | Swallow a watermelon seed and a watermelon grows in your tummy. | Seeds need soil, water and sunlight to grow. A swallowed seed just passes through. | Cleveland Clinic |
| ⏱️ | Food dropped on the floor is safe if you pick it up within five seconds. | Scientists found germs can jump onto dropped food in less than one second. Wet food like watermelon picks them up fastest. | Rutgers University |

### The Pond (+8)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🎬 | All frogs say "ribbit". | Most frogs don't! "Ribbit" is the call of a few tree frogs from America's west coast. Films used their recording for every frog, so we think they all sound like that. | AP News |
| 🦛 | Hippos sweat blood. | Hippos make a red-orange goo that works like sunscreen and helps keep germs away. It isn't blood, and it isn't sweat. | Nature |
| 🦫 | Beavers eat fish. | Beavers are vegetarians. They munch bark, leaves, roots and water plants. | National Wildlife Federation |
| 🔌 | Electric eels are eels. | Electric eels are knifefish, closer cousins of catfish than of real eels. They even swim up to gulp air. | Smithsonian |
| 🦟 | All mosquitoes bite. | Only female mosquitoes bite. They need blood to make their eggs. Males sip nectar from flowers. | Smithsonian |
| 🏊 | You must wait an hour after eating before you swim. | Lifesaving experts found no case of anyone getting into trouble because they swam after a meal. A big lunch might just make you feel lazy. | American Red Cross |
| 🛟 | Pool water changes colour if someone pees in it. | There is no such dye. Pools tell that story so nobody tries it. | Snopes |
| 👁️ | Chlorine on its own is what makes your eyes red in the pool. | Red eyes come from chlorine mixing with the pee, sweat and dirt that swimmers bring in. A shower before you swim helps. | CDC |

### The Library (+11)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🕯️ | Reading in dim light ruins your eyes. | It can make your eyes tired, but it does no lasting harm. A good lamp just makes reading comfier. | American Academy of Ophthalmology |
| 📺 | Sitting close to the TV damages your eyes. | It doesn't. Children can focus up close better than grown-ups, so they often sit near. Eyes can still get tired, so take breaks. | American Academy of Ophthalmology |
| 🩸 | The blood in your veins is blue. | Blood is always red: dark red in your veins, bright red in your arteries. Veins only look blue because of how light shines through your skin. | Scientific American |
| 👯 | Identical twins have the same fingerprints. | Fingerprints form from tiny bumps and pushes before you're born, so even identical twins have different ones. | Scientific American |
| 🦷 | George Washington had wooden teeth. | His false teeth were made of ivory, metal, and human and animal teeth. Never wood. | Mount Vernon |
| 🎂 | Queen Marie Antoinette said "Let them eat cake." | There's no proof she ever said it. A writer told the story about an unnamed princess while Marie Antoinette was still a child far away in Austria. | Britannica |
| 🪟 | Old windows are thicker at the bottom because glass slowly flows. | Glass is a solid. Old window panes were made unevenly, and builders usually put the thick edge at the bottom. | Scientific American |
| 🦖 | Cave people lived alongside dinosaurs. | The big dinosaurs died out about 66 million years before the first people. Birds are dinosaurs, though, so you see some every day! | Natural History Museum |
| 🦕 | "Dinosaur" means "big lizard". | It means "terrible lizard". A scientist called Richard Owen made the word up in 1842. | Natural History Museum |
| 🌹 | "Ring a Ring o' Roses" is about the plague. | The rhyme shows up in books hundreds of years after the plague, and nobody linked the two until the 1900s. Experts on old rhymes say it's just a dancing game. | Library of Congress |
| 🗣️ | Learning two languages at once confuses young children. | Children all over the world grow up with two or more languages. Mixing words for a while is normal, and they sort it out. | American Speech-Language-Hearing Association |

### The Ocean (+9)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🐚 | You can hear the sea in a seashell. | You hear the noises around you, bouncing about inside the shell. A cup over your ear does the same trick. | Scientific American |
| 🎐 | Jellyfish are fish. | Jellyfish have no bones, no brain and no heart. They are mostly water, and they were drifting in the sea long before the dinosaurs. | NOAA |
| 🩹 | Peeing on a jellyfish sting makes it better. | It doesn't help, and it can make the sting worse. Rinse it with seawater and get a grown-up. | Scientific American |
| 🐳 | Whales are fish. | Whales are mammals, like us. They breathe air, have warm blood and feed their babies milk. | NOAA |
| 🐬 | Dolphins never sleep. | Dolphins sleep with half their brain at a time. The other half keeps them breathing and watching for danger. | Smithsonian |
| 💙 | The sea is blue only because it reflects the sky. | Water itself soaks up red light and leaves the blue. That's why deep, clean water looks blue even on a cloudy day. | NOAA |
| 🦑 | An octopus has eight tentacles. | An octopus has eight arms. Tentacles are longer, with suckers only at the tip. Squid have two tentacles as well as eight arms. | Smithsonian |
| 🪨 | Coral is a rock or a plant. | Coral is made of tiny animals called polyps. Each builds a stony cup around itself, and millions together make a reef. | NOAA |
| 🏴‍☠️ | Pirates buried their treasure on islands. | Real pirates almost never buried treasure. They spent or shared it quickly. The idea comes mostly from the book "Treasure Island". | Smithsonian |

### The Desert (+8)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🎶 | You can tell a rattlesnake's age by counting its rattle. | A rattlesnake adds a new piece each time it sheds its skin, which can be a few times a year, and old pieces break off. | Smithsonian |
| 🥚 | Snakes unhinge their jaws to swallow big meals. | A snake's jaw never comes apart. The two halves of its lower jaw are joined by stretchy tissue, so they spread wide. | Natural History Museum |
| 🏖️ | Deserts are mostly sand. | Most deserts are rock and gravel. Sand dunes cover only a small part, about a tenth to a fifth of the world's deserts. | USGS |
| 🌿 | The Sahara has always been a desert. | Thousands of years ago the Sahara was green, with lakes, grass, hippos and giraffes. People painted them on the rocks. | Smithsonian |
| 🏺 | Everyone who opened Tutankhamun's tomb was cursed. | Most of the team lived long lives. Howard Carter, who found the tomb, lived for more than fifteen years afterwards. | British Medical Journal |
| 🗿 | Napoleon's soldiers shot the nose off the Sphinx. | Drawings made in the 1730s, long before Napoleon's army arrived in 1798, already show the Sphinx without a nose. | Smithsonian |
| 🦅 | Vultures circle over an animal that is about to die. | Vultures circle to ride rising warm air while they look, and sniff, for food. They aren't waiting for anyone. | Audubon |
| 💎 | Diamonds are made from squashed coal. | Most diamonds formed deep underground from carbon, long before there were land plants to turn into coal. | Smithsonian |

### The Arctic (+8)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🧤 | Rubbing snow on frostbite warms it up. | Rubbing frozen skin hurts it more. Warm it slowly with body heat or warm (not hot) water, and get help. | NHS |
| 🐺 | Wolves howl at the Moon. | Wolves howl to call their pack and tell other wolves to keep away. They point their noses up so the sound carries far. | International Wolf Center |
| 🦄 | A narwhal's horn is a horn. | It's a tooth! A long spiral tusk that grows right through the narwhal's upper lip. | Smithsonian |
| 🐾 | Polar bears are all left-handed. | Scientists who watch polar bears found they use both paws. | Polar Bears International |
| 🏔️ | Icebergs are frozen seawater. | Icebergs break off glaciers, which are built from snow. So an iceberg is fresh water, even though it floats in the sea. | National Snow and Ice Data Center |
| 🌌 | It's dark all the time in the Arctic. | In winter it stays dark for weeks, but in summer the Sun doesn't set at all. It's called the midnight sun. | National Snow and Ice Data Center |
| 🦉 | Owls can turn their heads all the way round. | Owls turn their heads about three-quarters of the way round, 270 degrees. That's still far more than you can! | Johns Hopkins Medicine |
| ☃️ | It can be too cold to snow. | It can snow at almost any temperature below freezing. Very cold air just holds less water, so the snow is lighter. | Met Office |

### The Jungle (+10)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🐘 | Elephants are scared of mice. | There's no proof. Elephants can be startled by anything that moves suddenly near their feet, but mice aren't special. | Smithsonian |
| 🥤 | Elephants drink through their trunks like straws. | An elephant sucks water partway up its trunk, then squirts it into its mouth. | Smithsonian |
| 🦍 | Gorillas beat their chests with their fists. | Gorillas use cupped hands, which make a loud popping drum sound that carries a long way through the forest. | Dian Fossey Gorilla Fund |
| 🌳 | Rainforests make most of the air we breathe. | Rainforests make lots of oxygen, but they use most of it up too. About half of the world's oxygen comes from tiny ocean plants called plankton. | National Geographic |
| 🦁 | Lions live in the jungle. | Lions live on open grasslands called savannas. Tigers and leopards are the big cats you might meet in a jungle. | National Geographic |
| 🍍 | Pineapples grow on trees. | Each pineapple grows on top of a small, spiky plant on the ground, and takes about two years. | Britannica |
| 🥜 | Peanuts are nuts. | Peanuts are legumes, cousins of peas and beans. They grow underground. | Britannica |
| 🌩️ | Thunder is the sound of clouds bumping together. | Lightning heats the air hotter than the surface of the Sun in a flash. The air bursts outward with a BOOM. | NOAA |
| 🌲 | A tree is a safe place in a thunderstorm. | Tall trees attract lightning. Go inside a building or a car instead. | National Weather Service |
| 🌈 | A rainbow has exactly seven colours. | A rainbow has countless colours blending into each other. Isaac Newton picked seven to match the seven notes of a musical scale. | Met Office |

### The Night Sky (+11)

| | Myth | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌡️ | Mercury is the hottest planet because it's closest to the Sun. | Venus is hotter! Its thick clouds trap heat like a blanket, making it hot enough to melt lead. | NASA |
| 🍂 | Summer happens when Earth is closest to the Sun. | Seasons come from Earth's tilt. Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January, which is winter in the north! | NASA |
| 🌝 | The Moon makes its own light. | Moonlight is sunlight bouncing off the Moon's grey rocks. | NASA |
| 🕳️ | Black holes suck up everything like a vacuum cleaner. | From far away, a black hole pulls just like a star of the same weight. You'd have to get very close to fall in. | NASA |
| 💫 | The Sun is the biggest star there is. | The Sun is a medium-sized star. Some giant stars are so big that over a billion Suns could fit inside. | NASA |
| 🔴 | Mars is red because it's hot. | Mars is red because its dust is rusty. It's freezing cold there, colder than Antarctica on most days. | NASA |
| 💍 | Saturn is the only planet with rings. | Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have rings too. Saturn's are just the biggest and brightest. | NASA |
| 🌕 | A full moon makes people act strange. | Scientists have checked hospital and police records many times and found no more trouble on full-moon nights. | Scientific American |
| 🌄 | The Moon is bigger when it's near the horizon. | It's the same size. Take a photo of it low and high and compare: your brain is playing a trick. | NASA |
| 🔨 | Heavy things always fall faster than light things. | Without air in the way, everything falls at the same speed. An astronaut on the Moon dropped a hammer and a feather, and they landed together. | NASA |
| 🥕 | Eating carrots lets you see in the dark. | Carrots help keep eyes healthy, but they won't give you night vision. The story was spread in World War II to hide Britain's new radar. | Smithsonian |

## Left out on purpose

- **Contested science:** hot water freezing faster than cold, eight glasses of water a day, no two snowflakes alike.
- **Too grim for the age group:** what happens to hair and nails after death, the Salem trials, lobsters in pots.
- **Could upset some families:** human evolution.
- **Partly true, so a bad "myth":** goldfish growing to fit their tank, cats always landing on their feet.
