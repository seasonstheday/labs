You are the squirrel. Tap the lawn to bury an acorn. Far from the tree is best.
Game 1 is the boom-and-bust model from the video: lean years shrink the squirrels, a mast year swamps them, and the whole forest has to do it in the same year (a warm, dry spring) or the eaters just walk over from next door. Numbers are illustrative: a mature oak drops about 2,000 acorns in an ordinary year and up to 10,000 in a mast year; a squirrel eats or buries a few hundred a season. Game 2: squirrels forget some of what they bury, and an acorn under its parent is shaded out, so the forgotten ones far from the tree are the oaks of a hundred years from now.
